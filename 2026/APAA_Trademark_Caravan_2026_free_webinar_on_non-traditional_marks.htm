The topic

Non-Traditional Marks:
From Practice to Challenges

 

Can a colour, a shape or even a laugh be a trademark?

Brands are increasingly recognised by more than their names. Yet such signs are rarely seen as distinctive in themselves. Owners must often prove that the public has come to know them through use, and each jurisdiction approaches that question in its own way. Even once registered, these marks can be hard to enforce.

In this moderated panel, practitioners from Australia, Japan and Thailand share where their jurisdictions stand, then turn to the challenge they all share: judging whether a sign is distinctive in itself, proving that it has become distinctive through use, and protecting it once it is won.

Three jurisdictions. One conversation. Practical insights for anyone who protects brands across Asia Pacific.
